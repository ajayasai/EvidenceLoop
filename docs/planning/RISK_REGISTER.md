Status: PROPOSED. Probability is a qualitative planning estimate, not a measured forecast. P0 blocks a public/clinical claim or competition entry; P1 threatens core delivery; P2 affects optional value. Owners are roles until named people commit. A reviewer-unavailable synthetic fallback means internal health-workflow rehearsals; public demonstrations are limited to architecture or abstract nonclinical examples, with no health-assessment or clinical-performance claim.

## Escalation and operational decisions

Critical failure owner is the developer lead; scientific wording/interpretation owner is the qualified reviewer. The team lead owns eligibility and submission. These roles must be assigned at M0; the plan does not imply those people have agreed. A critical output, source-integrity or cross-owner access defect freezes the affected path until repaired and rechecked. A small passing test suite never permits bypassing clinical oversight for real users.

## Blueprint critique and concrete improvements

1. **Workload:** a three-person 255-hour plan cannot be inherited by one developer. Use the hour budget/cut dates and one first family.
2. **Approval versus implementation:** source review does not review every generated answer. Constrain renderer text and audit support independently.
3. **Ground-truth cost:** 120 cases plus expert labeling, user study and 20-30 sources is ambitious. Reduce to six target families/60 scenarios and maintain honest denominators.
4. **Recall novelty:** changing a hash and notifying everyone is fragile and potentially harmful. Retain exact dependencies and human materiality review; only a synthetic prototype before submission.
5. **Semantic testing:** requiring a changed route for every important edit rewards wrong behavior. Assert field/warrant deltas and preserve correctly unchanged routes.
6. **Scope creep from care tracking:** arbitrary goals, glucose readings and reminder schedules imply clinical management. Limit MVP to one professional question and self-reported follow-up.
7. **Authority appearance:** a polished receipt can overstate certainty. Show uncertainty, scope and AI-assisted origin alongside the conclusion; never make "reviewed sources" look like personalized clinical sign-off.
8. **Evidence sufficiency:** one credible patient-information page cannot validate every desired operator. Enable only operator/family combinations with exact approved mappings; use no-coverage outcomes elsewhere.
9. **Claims of real-world benefit:** simulated timelines and immediate comprehension cannot establish long-term self-care improvement. Separate functionality, engineering measures, formative observations and clinical outcomes.
10. **Backup illusion:** hardcoded full answers conceal failure. Replace only the model boundary in disclosed replay; preserve policy, evidence, version and ownership logic.
